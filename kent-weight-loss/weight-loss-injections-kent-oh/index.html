    How Weight Loss Injections Work in Kent, OH

    How Weight Loss Injections Work in Kent

    Most modern weight loss shots are GLP-1 medicines. These drugs mimic a gut hormone released after meals, keeping food in the stomach longer, telling the brain you are full and steadying blood sugar. Semaglutide targets one receptor. Tirzepatide acts on both GLP-1 and GIP receptors, which may explain its larger average effect in trials.

    Who Weight Loss Injections Are For

    FDA-approved injections are for adults with a BMI of 30 or higher. Adults with a BMI of 27 or more also qualify if they have a weight-related condition such as high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol or sleep apnea. They work alongside diet and exercise. Pregnancy and some thyroid cancer histories rule them out.

    Injection Dosing Schedule in Kent

    Every weekly injection starts low. Wegovy begins at 0.25 mg and climbs every four weeks through 0.5, 1 and 1.7 mg to a maintenance dose of 2.4 mg, though some people stay at 1.7 mg. Zepbound starts at 2.5 mg and rises in 2.5 mg steps to as high as 15 mg. Same day each week.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Appetite often drops in the first week. The first month tends to show small changes on the scale since the dose is low, while stomach upset clusters right after each shot. Large trials reported average losses near 15 percent with semaglutide 2.4 mg over 68 weeks and near 20 percent with tirzepatide 15 mg over 72 weeks. Results vary.

    Injection Side Effects in Kent

    Nausea is the classic one. Others include constipation, diarrhea, heartburn and a small red bump where the needle went in, mostly early and around dose increases. Eating slowly and skipping greasy food on shot day takes the edge off. Less common but serious problems include pancreatitis, gallstones and kidney strain from dehydration.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Screening starts with BMI and medical history. Your provider then reviews every medication you take, looking hardest at insulin, sulfonylureas and birth control pills, since tirzepatide can make oral contraceptives less reliable after dose changes. Medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 in your family rules out GLP-1 injections. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are too.

    Brand-Name vs Compounded Injections in Kent

    Wegovy and Zepbound are FDA-approved for weight management. Ozempic and Mounjaro share those ingredients but carry type 2 diabetes labels instead. Compounded versions of semaglutide and tirzepatide come from licensed compounding pharmacies and lack FDA approval, which means no FDA review of their safety, effectiveness or manufacturing. Some patients choose them for availability.
